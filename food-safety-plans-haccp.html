Start with applicability

Not every facility needs the same kind of plan.

“HACCP plan” and “Food Safety Plan” are not interchangeable labels. The applicable framework depends on the products you make, the processes you use, the regulator with jurisdiction, and any exemptions or modified requirements that apply to your facility.

We start by understanding the operation first, then build the plan around the requirements that actually apply.

FSMA / FDA Preventive Controls

Food Safety Plans

Covered facilities under FDA's Preventive Controls for Human Food rule generally require a written, facility-specific Food Safety Plan developed under the hazard analysis and risk-based preventive controls framework.

Our consulting support includes PCQI-led plan development and reassessment, with the plan structured around the facility rather than a generic template.

  • Hazard analysis
  • Preventive controls, when required
  • Monitoring and corrective-action procedures
  • Verification and validation support
  • Supply-chain controls, when applicable
  • Recall-plan integration, when applicable
Commodity / Agency-Specific HACCP

HACCP Plans

HACCP remains the applicable regulatory framework for a number of food operations, including FDA-regulated seafood and juice processors and USDA/FSIS-regulated meat and poultry establishments.

We develop and reassess HACCP systems around the hazards, control strategies, critical limits, monitoring, corrective action, verification, and records appropriate to the operation.

  • Product and process evaluation
  • Hazard analysis
  • Critical Control Point determination
  • Critical limits and monitoring
  • Corrective actions
  • Verification, validation, and records

Our approach

The plan has to work on the production floor.

A technically correct plan that does not match the way the facility actually operates becomes a paperwork problem. We focus on building controls, monitoring activities, records, and responsibilities that make sense in the real workflow while still meeting the applicable regulatory framework.

Operation-specific.
Products, processes, equipment, people, and workflows come first.
Practical documentation.
Forms and records should capture useful evidence, not create paperwork for its own sake.
Built for implementation.
The written plan and what actually happens in the facility should tell the same story.
